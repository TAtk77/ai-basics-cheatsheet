AI, Actually Explained

The handful of ideas and terms you need to follow any conversation about AI — no math, no jargon left unexplained.

01

The Family Tree

AI Artificial Intelligence — the broad goal: get a computer to do things that normally need human judgment.
Machine Learning A way to reach that goal — instead of programming rules by hand, the system learns patterns from examples.
Deep Learning Machine learning using neural networks — layered structures loosely inspired by neurons in a brain.
LLMs Large Language Models — deep learning trained on huge amounts of text to predict and generate language. This is what powers ChatGPT, Claude, and similar tools.
02

How a Chat With an LLM Works

You type A question or instruction — called a prompt.
→
Tokenize Text is chopped into small chunks (tokens) the model can process.
→
Predict The model predicts the most likely next token, over and over.
→
You get Tokens are strung back into words — the response.
03

Glossary

Model

The trained system itself — think of it as the finished "brain" you're talking to.

Training

The process of showing the model huge amounts of data so it learns patterns.

Parameters

The internal dials (often billions) the model tunes during training. Roughly, more = more capacity to learn nuance.

Prompt

The input you give the model — your question, instruction, or context.

Context window

How much text (prompt + conversation so far) the model can "see" at once before it starts forgetting the beginning.

Fine-tuning

Extra, more focused training on top of a base model to specialize it for a task or tone.

Hallucination

When a model states something false or made-up with full confidence. Always worth a sanity check.

Inference

The moment the trained model is actually used to generate an answer — as opposed to training it.

04

Getting Better Answers

01

Be specific. "Write a birthday message for my sister who loves hiking" beats "write a birthday message."

02

Give context, not just instructions. Tell it who the answer is for and what it's used for.

03

Treat it like a very well-read intern — sharp and fast, but double-check anything factual or high-stakes.

04

Iterate. The first answer is a draft, not a verdict — ask it to revise, shorten, or try a different angle.

05

10 Tools Worth Knowing

ChatGPT

The best-known general chatbot — writing, brainstorming, research, and answering questions.

Claude

An AI assistant (made by Anthropic) strong at writing, analysis, and helping with code.

Gemini

Google's AI assistant, built into Search, Gmail, and Docs for research and writing help.

Perplexity

An AI search engine that answers questions directly and links back to its sources.

Midjourney

Turns text descriptions into original images — popular for illustration and concept art.

Sora

Generates short video clips from a text prompt or a still image.

NotebookLM

Upload your own documents and ask questions — answers come only from what you gave it.

ElevenLabs

Generates realistic AI voices — text-to-speech, narration, and voice cloning.

Gamma

Turns a rough outline or notes into a polished slide deck or webpage in minutes.

GitHub Copilot

An AI that suggests and writes code as you type, right inside your code editor.
